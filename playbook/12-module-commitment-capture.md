# Fix 3: Capturing promises

- **Fixes:** things agreed in a text, on a call or out loud that never get written down; missed meetings; one of you having to be the other's reminder
- **Time:** about 40 minutes in total, plus two weeks of practice. Part 1: 5 min each. Part 2: 10 min each. Part 3: 10 min together. Part 4: 5 min together. Part 5: 10 min together, a week later
- **Who:** Parts 1 and 2 each partner does on their own phone. Parts 3, 4 and 5 are done together
- **Devices:** each person's iPhone
- **Before you start:** Fix 2 is done through Part 3, so Family and Business show on both phones
- **What changes:** new events land on a shared calendar with an alert, without anyone choosing a calendar each time. Each of you has one quick way to capture a promise the moment it's made
- **What stays the same:** the calendar app, and how you talk to each other and to clients
- **Undo:** two settings on each phone, each changed back in seconds. The habits are yours to drop
- **Last checked:** 2026-11-10, iOS 27.2, against Apple's iPhone guide

## Why this matters

Say something close to this:

"The shared calendars only help if promises actually get onto them. The gap is the ten seconds after you say 'sure, Thursday at three' and before you put the phone back in your pocket. This fix makes those ten seconds easy: one move, done by voice if you like, and the phone reminds you in time. The aim is that neither of you has to be the other's memory."

Be honest about what kind of fix this is: the settings take ten minutes, and the rest is a habit. Expect it to take a couple of weeks to stick, and expect slips.

## Before you start

## Parts and steps

### Part 1. Point the phone at the shared calendar (each person, 5 minutes)

Goal: any new event, however it is created, lands on a shared calendar and comes with an alert.

#### Step 1.1 Choose where new events go
- **Who / where:** each partner, on their iPhone
- **Why:** when you add an event by voice or in a hurry, the phone puts it on its "default" calendar, the one it uses unless told otherwise. Today that is probably a calendar only one of you can see.
- **Do:**
  1. Ask: "Are more of the things you forget family ones or business ones?" That calendar becomes their default. Each partner can choose differently.
  2. **Settings > Apps > Calendar > Default Calendar**, and pick **Family** or **Business**.
- **Check:** "Which calendar has the tick next to it now?" Reassure them: an event that lands on the wrong one of the two is still visible to both of you, and switching it takes two taps (open the event, tap **Calendar**).
- **Undo:** the same screen. Note the previous default in the Map so it can be put back.

#### Step 1.2 Make every event come with an alert
- **Who / where:** each partner, on their iPhone
- **Why:** an event with no alert is just a note. This makes the phone speak up without anyone remembering to set an alert each time.
- **Do:** **Settings > Apps > Calendar > Default Alert Times**.
  1. Tap **Events** and choose a lead time. Suggest 30 minutes for someone mostly at a desk and 1 hour for someone who is out and has to travel.
  2. Tap **All-Day Events** and choose the option for the day before.
  3. Turn on **Time to Leave**, which uses traffic to prompt them when an event has an address.
- **Check:** they create an event for later today called "Test", then open it. "Does it show an alert already filled in?" Delete the test.
- **Undo:** the same screen; set each back to **None**.

**Safe stopping point.** One Checklist line per person. Save the Map.

### Part 2. Pick a capture move and practise it (each person, 10 minutes)

Goal: each partner has one way of capturing a promise that they can do in ten seconds, and has done it three times.

Offer the moves below and let each partner pick the one that suits how they work. One move, practised, beats four they half remember. The partner who is out all day will usually want voice; the one at a desk may prefer typing.

#### Step 2.1 Choose the move
- **Who / where:** each partner
- **Why:** the best capture method is the one they will actually use while walking to the car.
- **Do:** describe the options briefly, then ask which sounds most like them.
  - **A. Say it to Siri.** "Hey Siri, add site meeting Thursday at 3 p.m." It goes on the default calendar with the default alert. Hands-free, works in the car.
  - **B. Type it the way you'd say it.** In Calendar, tap **+** and type "Dentist Tuesday 9am" in the title. On recent iPhones the date and time fill themselves in.
  - **C. Straight from a text message.** When a message contains a day or time, it often appears underlined. Touch it and choose the option to create an event. *(The wording of that option varies between iOS versions; unconfirmed.)*
  - **D. Tell the assistant.** Only if they agreed in Fix 2, step 7.2 that the assistant may add events. They say or type "add lunch with the supplier, Friday at noon, to Business", and approve it on screen. *(How well this works by voice on the phone is unconfirmed; try it before relying on it.)*
- **Check:** each partner has named one move as theirs. Record it in the Map.
- **Undo:** they can switch moves any time.

#### Step 2.2 Practise three times
- **Who / where:** each partner, on their iPhone
- **Why:** doing it three times now is what makes it available under pressure later.
- **Do:** give them three realistic made-up promises, one at a time, in the kind of words they'd really hear. For example: "Can you meet the inspector at the property Wednesday at ten?", "Pickup is at 4:15 on Friday", "Call the accountant back tomorrow afternoon". After each, they capture it with their move.
- **Check:** after each one: "Open Calendar. Is it there, on the right day and time, with an alert, on a shared calendar?" If Siri misheard, show them that fixing it is a tap on the event. Then have the other partner confirm they can see it too.
- **Undo:** delete the three practice events.

#### Step 2.3 A promise with no time
- **Who / where:** each partner
- **Why:** "I'll send it this week" is where most dropped promises live.
- **Do:** follow Decision 3 in the Map. With the usual agreement: when a promise has no time, they give it one by asking themselves "when will I actually do this?" and capture that. Practise once: "I'll get the quote over to you this week" becomes "Send quote, Thursday 4 p.m."
- **Check:** the practice item is on the calendar with a time and an alert.
- **Undo:** delete it.

**Safe stopping point.** One Checklist line per person. Save the Map.

### Part 3. Agree the house rule (both, 10 minutes)

Goal: one sentence both partners agree to, and a new job for the partner who has been the reminder.

#### Step 3.1 The rule
- **Who / where:** both
- **Why:** a shared rule means neither has to nag, and "is it on the calendar?" stops being a criticism.
- **Do:** propose this, and let them reword it until it sounds like them: **"If it has a time, it goes on the calendar before the phone goes back in the pocket. If it isn't on the calendar, it isn't agreed."** Record their version in the Map under *Agreed house rule*.
- **Check:** each partner says the rule in their own words.
- **Undo:** it's their rule; they can change it.

#### Step 3.2 Retire the human reminder
- **Who / where:** both
- **Why:** as long as one partner quietly catches everything, the other never needs the calendar, and the system depends on one tired person.
- **Do:** name it kindly. If the Map says one of them acts as the other's reminder, agree that for the next two weeks that partner stops reminding and asks one question instead: "Is it on the calendar?" The answer is either "yes" or "doing it now". Make clear this is a two-week experiment, and that genuinely important things still get a nudge.
- **Check:** both say yes to the two-week experiment.
- **Undo:** they go back to how it was.

#### Step 3.3 Paper notes
- **Who / where:** the partner who keeps paper notes, if either does
- **Why:** paper is a fine place to jot something down. It is a poor place for it to stay.
- **Do:** agree one moment each working day, such as the end of the day at the desk, when anything with a date on the notepad gets entered into the calendar. They keep the paper.
- **Check:** they name the moment.
- **Undo:** not needed.

#### Step 3.4 Check that an alert reaches both of you
- **Who / where:** both, with both phones
- **Why:** each phone, and each Google account, keeps its own alert settings. An event one of you adds may alert only the person who added it, and the one doing the pickup may be the other one.
- **Do:** partner A adds an event to **Family** for later today, called "Alert test". Partner B opens that event on their own phone and looks at its alert line.
- **Check:** "Does the event show an alert on B's phone too?" If it does, swap roles and repeat once. If it doesn't: B opens the event and adds an alert by hand to prove it can be done, and then, at a computer, each partner sets a standard alert for the Family and Business calendars in their own account at **calendar.google.com**, in each calendar's settings under event notifications. *(Wording unconfirmed.)* Until that is done, the house rule gains a second half: "and if it's for the other person, tell them."
- **Undo:** delete the test events; remove the notification in the calendar's settings.

**Safe stopping point.** Save the Map.

### Part 4. The look-ahead (both, 5 minutes to set up)

Goal: each of them looks at tomorrow once a day, and at the coming week together once a week.

#### Step 4.1 The daily minute
- **Who / where:** each partner
- **Why:** an alert thirty minutes before is too late for something that needed preparing. One look the evening before catches it.
- **Do:** each picks a moment that already exists in their day (after dinner, when the phone goes on the charger) to open Calendar and read tomorrow. If their assistant can read the calendar, they can ask it "what's on Family and Business tomorrow?", but it only sees what is in their Google account. Schedules they follow on the phone, like a team calendar, are not included unless they did the optional step 4.4 in Fix 2, so the phone's own calendar is the complete picture.
- **Check:** each has named their moment.
- **Undo:** not needed.

#### Step 4.2 The weekly ten minutes
- **Who / where:** both
- **Why:** this is where clashes, pickups and who-is-where get sorted before they become a rescue call.
- **Do:** they pick a regular time and add a repeating weekly event to **Family** called something like "Week ahead". In it they look at the next seven days together, including the kids' schedules.
- **Check:** the repeating event is on both phones.
- **Undo:** delete the repeating event.

**Safe stopping point.** Save the Map. Set *Where we left off* to Part 5, with the date one week from now.

### Part 5. One-week check (both, 10 minutes, a week later)

Goal: find out whether it is working, and adjust it until it is.

#### Step 5.1 Look back at the week
- **Who / where:** both
- **Why:** the first version of a habit rarely fits. One adjustment at a week is what makes it last.
- **Do:** ask, one at a time:
  1. "Was anything missed or nearly missed this week?" If so, walk back through it without blame: was it captured? If not, what was happening at the moment it was agreed?
  2. "How many times did one of you have to remind the other?"
  3. "Is your capture move working, or does it feel like a chore?" Offer a different move from step 2.1 if so.
  4. "Are the alerts arriving at a useful time, too early, or too late?" Adjust step 1.2.
  5. "Is the default calendar the right one?" Adjust step 1.1.
- **Check:** each change they chose has been made on the phone, not just talked about.
- **Undo:** each adjustment is the same setting again.

#### Step 5.2 Decide about the experiment
- **Who / where:** both
- **Why:** the two-week experiment from step 3.2 needs an ending, so it doesn't just fade.
- **Do:** the experiment ends two weeks after step 3.2, which is usually a week after this check. Write that date into the Map's *Where we left off* note now. On or after it, ask both whether the "is it on the calendar?" arrangement stays, changes, or goes.
- **Check:** the Map records what they decided, or the date it will be decided.
- **Undo:** not needed.

#### Step 5.3 Put the monthly checkup on the calendar
- **Who / where:** either partner, on their iPhone
- **Why:** a tidy setup drifts. Ten minutes a month keeps it, and it only happens if it is on the calendar.
- **Do:** add a repeating monthly event to **Family** called "10-minute checkup". When it comes round, they open the project and say they want to do the monthly checkup; you then run `20-monthly-checkup.md`.
- **Check:** the repeating event shows on both phones. Tick the line under *Monthly checkup* in the Checklist.
- **Undo:** delete the repeating event.

**Safe stopping point.** Save the Map. If the reminder experiment hasn't reached its end date, *Where we left off* points at step 5.2 with that date; otherwise the three fixes are complete, and the next sitting is the first monthly checkup.

## If your screen doesn't match

- **No "Apps" in Settings.** On older iOS versions, **Calendar** is directly in Settings. Use the Settings search for "Default Calendar".
- **Family and Business aren't offered as the default.** The Google account's calendars aren't on this phone yet. Go back to Fix 2, Part 3.
- **Siri put the event on a different calendar.** Check step 1.1 on that phone. Siri uses the default; asking it for a calendar by name is not reliable.
- **Siri isn't responding or is switched off.** Use move B instead; don't spend the sitting on Siri's settings.
- **Typing "Dentist Tuesday 9am" doesn't fill in the time.** That feature needs a recent iPhone. They set the day and time by hand below the title, or use Siri.
- **No underlined date in the text message.** It doesn't always appear. Use Siri or typing; they can read the message's details aloud to Siri.
- **The assistant says it can't add events.** The setting from Fix 2, step 7.2 isn't on, or the connection needs signing in again. See the assistant notes file.
- **No "Time to Leave".** It depends on the phone's settings for location. Skip it.

Anything else: follow the general routine in `00-start-here.md`.

## Undo

- **Default calendar:** **Settings > Apps > Calendar > Default Calendar**, back to the one noted in the Map.
- **Default alerts:** **Settings > Apps > Calendar > Default Alert Times**, each back to **None**.
- **The weekly event:** delete it.
- **The house rule and the reminder experiment:** theirs to change or end.

Nothing in this module removes anything.

## Done when

- A new event created by voice on either phone lands on a shared calendar with an alert, and the other partner can see it.
- Each partner has a named capture move and has used it for real, not just in practice.
- The house rule is in the Map in their own words.
- The weekly look-ahead is a repeating event on Family.
- An event added by one partner shows an alert on the other's phone.
- The one-week check has happened and its adjustments are made.
- The monthly checkup is a repeating event on Family.
- They can each describe the last promise they captured.
