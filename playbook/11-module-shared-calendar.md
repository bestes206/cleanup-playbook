# Fix 2: Shared calendars

- **Fixes:** one of you knowing the schedule and the other finding out by phone call; kids' activities visible on one phone only; a calendar list so cluttered that nothing in it can be trusted
- **Time:** about 90 minutes in total, in short sittings. Part 1: 10 min each. Part 2: 15 min. Part 3: 10 min each. Part 4: 10 min per schedule. Part 5: 10 to 15 min. Part 6: 10 min now and 5 min two weeks later. Part 7: 10 min each
- **Who:** Part 2 is done at a computer by the owner of each calendar's account, or with that owner sitting beside whoever is at the keyboard. Parts 1, 3 and 7 are done by each partner on their own devices. Parts 4, 5 and 6 either can do, with the other's yes for anything removed
- **Devices:** each person's iPhone. A computer with a web browser for Part 2 (it cannot be done in the phone's calendar app), and possibly for one fix-up in Part 3 and the optional last step of Part 4
- **Before you start:** the tools agreement in the Map names the calendar home, who owns the Family and Business calendars, and each partner's main Google account. Fix 1 has reached at least Part 4 for those Google accounts. Part 4 alone needs none of this and can be done early
- **What changes:** two new shared calendars, Family and Business, appear in the calendar app you already use. Schedules you follow show on both phones. Old clutter gets hidden
- **What stays the same:** the Apple Calendar app, your existing events, and how you add an event
- **Undo:** new calendars can be hidden or removed. Hidden calendars come back with one tick. Nothing old is deleted in this module without a backup and a yes
- **Last checked:** 2026-11-10, iOS 27.2, against Apple and Google help pages

The steps are written for the usual agreement: **Google stores the shared calendars, and you look at them in Apple Calendar.** If the Map says iCloud is the home, see the end of this file.

## Why this matters

Say something close to this:

"Right now each of you has your own picture of the week, and the only way they match is by one of you telling the other. After this, there's one Family calendar and one Business calendar that you both see and both add to, in the same calendar app you use today. The kids' schedules show up on both phones on their own. And because the Family and Business calendars will live somewhere your assistant can read, you'll be able to ask it what's on them tomorrow for both of you."

One limit to be honest about from the start: schedules they follow, like a team calendar, show on their phones but are not visible to the assistant unless they take the optional step at the end of Part 4.

## Before you start

Check the Map:

- **The agreement** names the calendar home, the owner account for each shared calendar, and each partner's main Google account. If any is missing, settle it with both partners first (`03-tools-agreement.md`, Decision 2).
- **Those Google accounts** are in the password app with their own passwords and the second sign-in step on. If not, do Part 4 of `10-module-password-manager.md` for them first: this module signs in to them on several devices, and that should happen once, with the right password.
- **A partner with no Google account of their own** creates a free one now, themselves, at accounts.google.com, saving its password straight into the password app. Record it in the Map as their main Google account. Tell them this account has its own inbox at gmail.com, which they'll need once, in step 2.3.
- **Never use an account marked `managed by someone else`** as an owner or a main account, even if it happens to be a Google account.
- **Part 4 has no prerequisites.** If the kids' schedule being on one phone is their top pain, it can be done before anything else in this module, straight after the tools agreement.

## Parts and steps

### Part 1. See what we have (each person, 10 minutes)

Goal: a list of every calendar on each phone, written into the Map. Nothing changes in this part.

#### Step 1.1 List the calendars
- **Who / where:** each partner, on their own iPhone
- **Why:** you can't tidy or share what you haven't listed, and the person with a dozen calendars often doesn't know what half of them are.
- **Do:** open **Calendar** and tap **Calendars** at the bottom. The list is grouped under headings (for example **iCloud**, **Subscribed**, **Other**, or an email address). Have them read out each heading and the calendar names under it, a few at a time.
- **Check:** read the list back. For each calendar ask two quick questions: "Do you know what this one is?" and "Is anything in it still happening?" `not sure yet` is fine.
- **Undo:** nothing changed.

#### Step 1.2 Sort them
- **Who / where:** you, with the partner confirming
- **Why:** each kind of calendar gets handled differently later.
- **Do:** add each calendar to the Map with one of these in its *For* and *Status* lines:
  - **Ours, in use:** they add events to it and still do.
  - **A schedule we follow:** published by someone else (a team, a school, a club). It usually sits under a **Subscribed** heading. They can see it but not change it.
  - **Don't recognise or looks old:** mark `not sure yet`. Part 6 deals with these.
- **Check:** the count in the Map matches the count on the phone.
- **Undo:** nothing changed on the phone; correct the Map if a calendar was sorted wrongly.

**Safe stopping point.** Save the Map.

### Part 2. Create the Family and Business calendars and share them (15 minutes, on a computer)

Goal: two new calendars exist, each owned by the account named in the agreement, and each shared with both partners' main Google accounts.

Say first: "This part needs a computer, because Google only lets you create a new calendar in a web browser."

Who sits at the computer matters. Each calendar is created while signed in as its owner account, and signing in needs that account's password from the password app and the sign-in code from its owner's phone. So the person whose account it is does the signing in, or sits beside whoever is at the keyboard. Nobody reads a password or a code out for the other to type into this chat. If the owner isn't available, this part waits for them.

#### Step 2.1 Create the Family calendar
- **Who / where:** the owner of the Family calendar's account, at the computer, in a web browser
- **Why:** a calendar belongs to the account that creates it, so it matters which account is signed in.
- **Do:**
  1. Go to **calendar.google.com** and sign in with the account the agreement names as the Family calendar's owner, letting the password app fill in the password.
  2. In the left-hand panel, next to **Other calendars**, click **+**, then **Create new calendar**.
  3. Name it **Family** and create it.
- **Check:** "Family" now appears in the left-hand panel under **My calendars**.
- **Undo:** the calendar's settings page has **Remove calendar**. An empty calendar can be removed without loss.

#### Step 2.2 Share it with your partner
- **Who / where:** same partner, same screen
- **Why:** sharing is what lets both of you see and add to it.
- **Do:**
  1. Hover over **Family** in the left panel, click the three dots, then **Settings and sharing**.
  2. Under **Shared with**, click **Add people and groups** and type the other partner's main Google address from the Map.
  3. For the permission, choose the option that lets them **make changes and manage sharing**; if that isn't offered, **Make changes and see event details**. Click **Send**.
  4. If the owner account is not this partner's own main Google account, add their own main address the same way.
- **Check:** both partners' main Google addresses are listed under **Shared with**.
- **Undo:** remove a person from the same list.

#### Step 2.3 Accept the invitation
- **Who / where:** the other partner, on their phone or a computer
- **Why:** the calendar isn't added to their account until they accept.
- **Do:** open the email from Google Calendar in the mailbox for their main Google address and tap the link in it to add the calendar. If their main Google account is brand new, that mailbox isn't on their phone: they open **gmail.com** in the phone's browser and sign in as the new account to find the email.
- **Check:** "Did it say the calendar was added?"
- **Undo:** they can remove the calendar from their own list at any time; this does not delete it.

#### Step 2.4 Do the same for the Business calendar
- **Who / where:** as steps 2.1 to 2.3
- **Why:** keeping business separate means it can later be shared with staff, or handed over, without the family calendar coming along.
- **Do:** if the Business calendar has a different owner account, click the round profile picture at the top right of calendar.google.com, choose **Add another account**, and sign in as that owner (its owner does the signing in). Then repeat steps 2.1 to 2.3 with the name **Business**.
- **Check:** each partner's main Google account can now see both calendars at calendar.google.com.
- **Undo:** as above.

**Safe stopping point.** Save the Map: both calendars recorded with owner, seen by, and can be changed by.

### Part 3. Show them on every device (each person, 10 minutes)

Goal: Family and Business appear in Apple Calendar on each phone and tablet, and a change made on one shows up on the other.

#### Step 3.1 Add the Google account to the iPhone's calendar
- **Who / where:** each partner, on their iPhone, then iPad
- **Why:** this is the piece of plumbing that makes Apple Calendar show Google calendars. Changes go both ways.
- **Do:**
  1. **Settings > Apps > Calendar > Calendar Accounts.**
  2. If their main Google account is already listed (because its mail is on the phone), tap it and turn on **Calendars**.
  3. If it isn't listed: **Add Account > Google**, sign in on Google's screen with the password app filling in the password, and turn on **Calendars**. Leave **Mail** and the other switches off unless they want that mailbox on this phone; nothing about their mail needs to change here.
- **Check:** open **Calendar**, tap **Calendars**. "Do you see a new heading with your Google address, and Family and Business under it?" Give it a minute and pull down on the list to refresh. If the account is there but one or both calendars are not, do step 3.2. If both are there, skip to step 3.3.
- **Undo:** turn the **Calendars** switch off for that account. The calendars disappear from the phone and are untouched at Google.

#### Step 3.2 Only if Family or Business is missing: tick it on Google's sync page
- **Who / where:** the partner whose phone is missing a calendar, signed in to their main Google account in a web browser, ideally on a computer
- **Why:** Google sends your own calendars to the iPhone's calendar app automatically, but one that someone else shared with you may need ticking once. *(Inferred from Google's help page, which describes the page for computers. It may also work in the phone's browser; unconfirmed.)*
- **Do:** go to **calendar.google.com/calendar/syncselect**. Tick **Family** and **Business**, and save. Back on the phone, pull down on the Calendars list to refresh.
- **Check:** "Are Family and Business both in the list on your phone now?"
- **Undo:** untick them on the same page.

#### Step 3.3 The round-trip test
- **Who / where:** both, phones side by side if possible, otherwise by text
- **Why:** this is the moment it becomes real.
- **Do:**
  1. Partner A creates an event tomorrow called "Test from A", and in the event's **Calendar** line picks **Family**.
  2. Partner B opens Calendar and looks at tomorrow. Allow a few minutes.
  3. Partner B taps the event, changes its time by an hour, and saves.
  4. Partner A looks again.
- **Check:** "Do you both see the same event at the new time?" Then delete the test event; it was made for this purpose, and either of them can do it.
- **Undo:** not needed.

If the event takes more than about fifteen minutes to cross: **Settings > Apps > Calendar > Calendar Accounts > Fetch New Data**, and set the schedule to the shortest interval offered. Pulling down on the Calendars list also forces a refresh.

#### Step 3.4 On the PC
- **Who / where:** whoever uses a PC
- **Why:** the same calendars, on the big screen, with nothing to install.
- **Do:** bookmark **calendar.google.com** in the browser, signed in to their main Google account.
- **Check:** Family and Business are listed and show the same events as the phone. Tell them: the schedules they follow on their phones will not appear here unless they do the optional step 4.4.
- **Undo:** delete the bookmark.

**Safe stopping point.** One Checklist line per person. Save the Map.

### Part 4. Bring in the schedules we follow (10 minutes per schedule)

Goal: every schedule that matters, such as a kids' team or a school calendar, shows on both partners' phones without anyone passing it on.

For each calendar marked **a schedule we follow** in the Map, starting with the one that has caused the most missed pickups:

#### Step 4.1 Get a fresh link
- **Who / where:** whoever knows where the schedule comes from
- **Why:** a schedule you follow is a link to someone else's calendar. Getting a fresh link from the source also proves the schedule is still alive.
- **Do:** go to the source (the team's app or website, the school's calendar page) and look for words like **Subscribe**, **Sync to calendar**, **Add to calendar** or **Calendar feed**. Copy the link it gives. If the source can't be found, the partner whose phone already shows the schedule can look for its address under **Calendars > (i)** next to it. *(Whether the address is shown there is unconfirmed.)*
- **Check:** they have a link copied. Some of these links are private to the family, so it should not go in the Map or the chat. If they want to keep it, a note in the password app's shared box is the place.
- **Undo:** nothing changed.

#### Step 4.2 Follow it on each phone
- **Who / where:** each partner, on their own iPhone. The first partner sends the link to the second by text
- **Why:** each person follows the schedule themselves, stored with their iCloud account so that it also appears on their iPad.
- **Do:** in **Calendar**, tap **Calendars > Add Calendar > Add Subscription Calendar**. Paste the link, tap the button to find it, and where it asks which **Account**, choose **iCloud**. Finish with **Done**. Explain: "This is a schedule you follow. You'll see every game and practice, and it updates itself, but you can't change the events, because the team publishes them."
- **Check:** "Look at the next two weeks. Do the next game or practice times look right on both phones?"
- **Undo:** **Calendars > (i)** next to it **> Unsubscribe**. It can be followed again from the same link.

Tell them two things to expect. Changes made by the team can take some hours to appear, so for a same-day change the team's own app or message is still the fastest source. And a schedule followed this way shows on their Apple devices only: it will not appear at calendar.google.com on the PC, and the assistant cannot see it. Step 4.4 is the optional way round that.

#### Step 4.3 The partner who already had it
- **Who / where:** the partner whose phone showed this schedule before
- **Why:** they may now see it twice, once from the old link and once from the new.
- **Do:** if it shows twice, untick the older one in **Calendars** (hide, don't remove). Part 6 handles removal.
- **Check:** each game appears once.
- **Undo:** tick it again.

#### Step 4.4 Optional: let the PC and the assistant see it too
- **Who / where:** each partner who wants it, at a computer, signed in to their main Google account
- **Why:** the assistant reads the calendar through each person's Google account, so a schedule that lives only on the phones is invisible to it, and to the PC. Without this step, "what's on tomorrow?" will leave out the kids' games.
- **Do:** at **calendar.google.com**, next to **Other calendars**, click **+**, then **From URL**, paste the same link, and add it. It can take up to a day to fill in, and it refreshes more slowly than the phone does. It will not show twice on the phone.
- **Check:** the next day, the schedule's events appear at calendar.google.com.
- **Undo:** in the calendar's settings at calendar.google.com, remove it.

Whether or not they do step 4.4, record it in the Map under *The assistant*, on the line for what it cannot see.

**Safe stopping point after each schedule.** Record each one in the Map with *Seen by: both*.

### Part 5. Move upcoming events across (10 to 15 minutes)

Goal: the things actually coming up in the next couple of months are on Family or Business, where both can see them.

Explain the approach: "We only move what's ahead of you, and one at a time. Everything already in your old calendars stays exactly where it is."

#### Step 5.1 Move what matters
- **Who / where:** each partner, on their iPhone
- **Why:** an appointment that only one of you can see is the problem this whole fix exists to solve.
- **Do:** look through the next eight weeks. For each event the other partner should know about: tap it, tap **Calendar** in the event's details, and pick **Family** or **Business**. Personal things that concern only one of them stay put.
- **Check:** the other partner opens their phone and reads out two of the moved events.
- **Undo:** tap the event and switch its **Calendar** line back.

Repeating events and events with invited guests can behave differently when moved. If one asks a question they don't understand, leave that event where it is and note it for the helper.

If there are far too many to move by hand, stop. A bulk move exists but is done on a computer and can create duplicates; it is a job for the helper. Add it to *Questions for the helper*.

**Safe stopping point.** Save the Map.

### Part 6. Tidy the old calendars (10 minutes now, 5 minutes two weeks later)

**Removal step.** This part can end in deleting things, so it goes in two stages with a two-week gap. Stage one only hides. Hiding is fully reversible, and for most households hiding is enough.

#### Step 6.1 Decide which ones are dead
- **Who / where:** the partner with the cluttered list, on their iPhone
- **Why:** there is no label that says a calendar is dead, so you look at what's in it.
- **Do:** for each calendar marked `not sure yet` or looking old: in **Calendars**, untick everything except that one, tap **Done**, and look ahead through the next three months, then back through the last six. Then sort it:
  - **Live:** it has events ahead, or the activity is still going. Keep.
  - **Sleeping:** nothing ahead, but it will return (a sports season, a school year). Hide until then.
  - **Dead:** nothing ahead, nothing in the last six months, and the activity is over. Hide now; a candidate for removal later.
- **Check:** every calendar in the Map now has a status. Tick the calendars back on that should be showing.
- **Undo:** nothing changed yet.

#### Step 6.2 Hide them
- **Who / where:** same partner
- **Why:** hiding gives the clean view today, with nothing lost.
- **Do:** in **Calendars**, untick each sleeping and dead calendar, then **Done**. Record `hidden since <date>` in the Map.
- **Check:** "Does your calendar look clearer? Is anything missing that you expected to see?" If something is missing, tick that calendar back on and mark it live.
- **Undo:** tick it again.

**Safe stopping point.** Save the Map. Tell them the next step is in two weeks and is optional. Put a note in *Where we left off* with the date.

#### Step 6.3 Two weeks later: remove, or leave hidden
- **Who / where:** both partners
- **Why:** two weeks of not missing a calendar is the evidence that it's safe to let go. Leaving it hidden forever is also a perfectly good outcome.
- **Do:** ask whether anything was missed while the calendars were hidden. For each dead calendar they want gone, the backup and the yes come first:
  - **A schedule they follow:** the events belong to whoever publishes it, so nothing of theirs is lost. The way back is following it again from the source. With a yes from both: **Calendars > (i) > Unsubscribe**.
  - **A calendar of their own:** leave it hidden. Treat every calendar they created as having events in it, however empty it looked in step 6.1, because that step only looked a few months each way and old calendars hold years of history. Deleting one deletes every event in it, on every device, and for anyone it was shared with. It needs a saved copy first, and making that copy takes a computer and a few careful steps. If they really want it gone, it is a job to do with the helper; add it to *Questions for the helper*. The one exception is a calendar created by mistake during this cleanup that they know has nothing in it.
- **Check:** the calendar list on the phone matches the Map. Each removal has a line in the Change log saying what was removed, when, and what the way back is.
- **Undo:** follow a schedule again from its source. For a deleted iCloud calendar, icloud.com has a **Data Recovery > Restore Calendars** option that can bring back an earlier snapshot, but it rolls back all calendars and resets sharing, so treat it as an emergency measure and involve the helper.

### Part 7. Let the assistant see the calendars (each person, 10 minutes)

Goal: each partner's assistant can read Family and Business, so "what's on tomorrow?" gets a true answer.

Only do this part if the agreement lists the calendar under *The assistant may be connected to*.

#### Step 7.1 Connect
- **Who / where:** each partner, in the assistant's own settings
- **Why:** each person's connection is their own; one partner connecting does nothing for the other.
- **Do:** follow the connection steps in the assistant notes file for Google Calendar, using their main Google account. They sign in on Google's own screen, with the password app filling in the password. Remind them you will not see it.
- **Check:** ask them to ask you, "What's on the Family calendar this week?" Read back what you find and have them compare with their phone. Then say plainly what you cannot see: any calendar that isn't in their Google account, which includes schedules they follow on the phone (unless they did step 4.4) and anything still in their old calendars. From now on, whenever you answer a question like "what's on tomorrow?", end by naming what you can't see, so a missing game is never mistaken for a free afternoon.
- **Undo:** the same settings screen has a disconnect option. Disconnecting changes nothing in the calendar.

#### Step 7.2 Decide whether the assistant may add events
- **Who / where:** both partners
- **Why:** reading is harmless. Adding is useful for capturing promises, but they should choose it on purpose.
- **Do:** ask whether they want to be able to say "add this to the calendar" to the assistant. If yes, the assistant notes file says whether anything has to be switched on first. Record the choice in the Map under *The assistant*. `12-module-commitment-capture.md` uses it.
- **Check:** if they said yes, have them ask you to add a test event to Family, approve it on screen, and find it on their phone. Then they delete it.
- **Undo:** the same setting, or disconnect.

**Safe stopping point.** Save the Map.

## If your screen doesn't match

- **No "Apps" in Settings.** Older iOS versions have **Calendar** directly in Settings, and the accounts under **Calendar > Accounts**. Use the Settings search for "Calendar Accounts".
- **The Google account is on the phone but Family and Business don't show.** Do step 3.2: the calendars may need ticking on Google's sync page. Then pull down on the Calendars list to refresh.
- **"Create new calendar" isn't there.** They are in the phone's Google Calendar app or the mobile website. It needs a computer browser. In a pinch, the phone's browser can request the desktop version of the site, but a computer is far less fiddly.
- **The invitation email never arrived.** Check which mailbox belongs to the address that was typed in step 2.2, and its spam folder. The owner can remove and re-add the person to send it again.
- **The permission choices have different wording.** Any option that says "make changes" works. Avoid the ones that only say "see".
- **An event shows twice.** They are seeing the same event from two calendars. Hide calendars one at a time in **Calendars** to find which two, then hide the older one.
- **Events show at the wrong hour.** Check **Settings > Apps > Calendar > Time Zone Override** is off, unless they turned it on deliberately.
- **Colours keep changing back.** For Google calendars the colour is set at calendar.google.com, not on the phone.
- **Adding the subscription link gives an error.** The link is old or was copied partly. Get a fresh one from the source.

Anything else: follow the general routine in `00-start-here.md`.

## Undo

- **The new calendars:** hide them on a phone with one tick, or switch off **Calendars** for the Google account. To remove a calendar entirely, its owner uses **Remove calendar** at calendar.google.com. That deletes its events for everyone, so move anything wanted back first, and treat it as a removal step.
- **A followed schedule:** unsubscribe, and follow again from the source any time.
- **Moved events:** switch each event's **Calendar** line back.
- **Hidden calendars:** tick them.
- **The assistant's connection:** disconnect in the assistant's settings.

## Done when

- Both partners see Family and Business in Apple Calendar on every phone and tablet they use, and the round-trip test passed.
- Whoever uses a PC can see the same calendars in a browser.
- Each schedule they follow shows on both phones, and they know whether the PC and the assistant can see it.
- Events in the next eight weeks that concern both of them are on Family or Business.
- Each partner can read out tomorrow from their own phone, and the two match.
- Every calendar in the Map has a status, and nothing was removed without a Change log line.
- If agreed, each partner's assistant can read the shared calendars.

## Update the Map

- *Calendars*: one block per calendar, with where it lives, seen by, can be changed by, and status.
- *Apple and Google accounts*: each partner's main Google account, if it was created or changed here.
- *The assistant*: what each partner has connected, whether it may add events, and what it cannot see.
- Checklist: Fix 2 parts, with dates and names.
- Change log: one line per sitting, and one per removal.

## If the agreed home is iCloud

The parts and goals stay the same. The differences:

- **Part 2** is done on an iPhone, no computer needed: **Calendar > Calendars > Add Calendar**, name it, then tap **(i)** next to it **> Add Person**, enter the partner's Apple Account email, and make sure **Allow Editing** is on. The partner accepts the notification on their phone. Skip steps 2.3 and 2.4, and step 3.2.
- **Part 3** needs no new account on the phones. On a PC, the calendars open at **icloud.com/calendar** in a browser. Skip step 4.4.
- **Part 7** depends on the assistant. Check the assistant notes file: where an iCloud connection exists, it signs in with a special one-off password created on Apple's website, which is typed into the assistant's settings screen and never into a chat. Whether shared calendars are visible through it is unconfirmed; test with the "what's on this week" question before relying on it.
