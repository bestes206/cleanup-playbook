# Intake: the interview that builds the Map

**For the assistant.** This is the first half of the joint session. You interview both partners and build their Map (format in `04-map-and-checklist.md`). The second half is the tools agreement (`03-tools-agreement.md`). Follow the ground rules in `00-start-here.md` throughout.

- **Who:** both partners, together. If only one is present, do not start; offer to help them pick a time when both can sit down. The whole point of this session is a shared foundation, and a map built from one person's memory will be wrong about the other's accounts.
- **Time:** about 30 to 40 minutes for the interview, then 15 to 20 for the tools agreement. Say this before you begin, and say there is a safe stopping point between the two.
- **Nothing changes in this session.** You are only writing things down. Tell them so; it lowers the stakes.

## How to interview

- One question per message. Wait for the answer before asking the next one.
- Plain words. If you must use a technical word, explain it in the same sentence.
- "I don't know" is a good answer. Write it in the Map as `not sure yet` and move on. Only look it up now if the next question depends on it, and then show them where to look, one tap at a time.
- When the two disagree or remember differently, write down both versions. Don't referee.
- Offer examples when a question is hard to answer cold ("for instance Gmail, Yahoo, Outlook, iCloud"). People recognise more than they recall.
- Never ask what a password is. You may ask where passwords are kept and whether one is reused.
- Don't offer fixes during the interview. If they ask "should we...?", note it in the Map under *Not sure yet* and say the tools agreement covers it. Mixing the two makes the interview twice as long.
- After each part, read back what you captured in two or three lines and ask "did I get that right?"
- About every ten minutes, tell them roughly how far along they are.

## Opening (say this in your own words)

1. What this is: a guided tidy-up of their accounts, calendars and passwords so that things stop falling through the cracks and their assistant can actually help.
2. Today is questions only. Nothing gets changed, moved or deleted.
3. The one firm rule: never type a password, PIN or security code into this chat, and you will never ask for one.
4. How long it takes, and that they can stop at any point without losing anything, because you save as you go.
5. Ask what each of them would like to be called. Use those names from here on.

## Part 1. What bugs you most (5 minutes)

Start here, before any inventory. It tells you what to fix first, and people open up more readily about a missed appointment than about account settings.

Ask each partner in turn:

1. "Think about the last couple of weeks. What went wrong or wasted your time because something was in the wrong place, missing, or not shared?" Ask for one real, recent example each.
2. "If one thing just worked from next month on, what would you want it to be?"

Write their answers in their own words. You will come back to these at the end to rank them.

## Part 2. Devices (3 minutes)

For each partner:

3. "Which devices do you use, and which one do you use most?" (phone, tablet, computer; iPhone or Android; Windows PC or Mac)
4. "Is any device shared between you, or used by the kids or staff?"

This decides which version of each later step you give them. Someone who does everything on an iPhone must never be sent to a computer without a phone alternative.

## Part 3. Email (8 minutes)

5. "Let's list every email address either of you uses. Start with the one you check most." For each address, ask the follow-ups below one at a time. Keep going until they run out, then ask "any old ones that still get mail, or that other accounts send reset emails to?"

For each address, capture:

- A short nickname they would recognise ("the leads inbox", "old family Yahoo").
- Who uses it: one of them, or both.
- What it is for: business, personal, or mixed.
- Who provides it (Gmail, Yahoo, Outlook, iCloud, a company or brokerage).
- **Who owns it.** "If you left the business, or sold it, would this address go with you or stay with the business?" and "Did an employer, brokerage or franchise give you this account?" If yes to the second, mark it `managed by someone else`. That flag matters later: those accounts often have rules about what may be connected to them.
- Where they read it (which app, which device).
- Whether mail from other addresses is forwarded into it.

Then ask:

6. "Which address do password-reset emails usually go to?" (If it differs by account, `varies` is fine.)

## Part 4. Apple and Google accounts (5 minutes)

Skip the Apple questions for anyone with no Apple devices, and the Google ones for anyone with no Google account.

7. For each partner with an iPhone or iPad: "Do you know which email address is your Apple Account, the one your phone is signed in with?" If not sure, this one is worth looking up now because several later steps depend on it. Walk them through it: open **Settings**, tap their name at the very top; the address shown under their name is the Apple Account. Record it. If more than one address is listed there, record which one is shown at the top.
8. "Do the two of you share one Apple Account on any device, or does each of you have your own?"
9. "Is Family Sharing set up?" (`not sure yet` is common here.)
10. For each partner: "Which Google account do you think of as your own main one, if any?" Note whether it is one of the shared business addresses or a personal one.

## Part 5. Calendars (6 minutes)

11. For each partner: "When you want to know what's happening tomorrow, where do you look?" (an app, paper, your partner)
12. "Which calendar app, and roughly how many calendars are listed inside it?" If they can easily check, have them open the app and tap **Calendars** at the bottom to count. A rough number is enough.
13. "Are any of those calendars shared between the two of you today?"
14. "Do you follow any schedules that someone else publishes, like a kids' sports team, a school, or a club?" Capture what each one is, who can see it now, and whether it still seems to be updating.
15. "Where do work appointments and client meetings go today, if anywhere?"
16. "Does anyone else need to see or add to your calendars, such as staff, a relative, or a sitter?"

## Part 6. Promises and to-dos (4 minutes)

17. For each partner: "When you agree to something in a text, on a call, or out loud, what do you do so you don't forget it?" Accept "nothing" without comment.
18. "Does either of you act as the other's reminder?"
19. "Do you use any to-do or reminders app, or paper?"

## Part 7. Passwords (4 minutes)

Remind them first: you are asking *where* passwords live, never *what* they are.

20. "Where do your passwords live today?" (memory, paper, sticky notes, a notes app, the phone's saved passwords, a password app)
21. "Is there one password, or a small set, that gets used on lots of accounts?" Record only yes or no, and whether both partners know it.
22. "Roughly how often does one of you get locked out of something?"
23. "Is a second sign-in step turned on for any account, where a code is sent to your phone?" (`not sure yet` is common.)
24. "If one of you were unreachable for a week, could the other get into the accounts that keep the household and the business running?"

## Part 8. The assistant (3 minutes)

25. "What have you already connected to this assistant, if anything?" (email, calendar, files)
26. "Once things are tidy, what would you most like to be able to ask it?" Get two or three concrete examples each ("what's on tomorrow for both of us", "what did I promise the client on Tuesday"). These become the test at the end of the pilot: can it actually answer them?

## Part 9. Parking lot (2 minutes)

These areas have no fix module yet. Capture one line each so nothing is lost, and say plainly that they come later.

27. "A few quick ones we'll park for a later round. Any pain with: too many notifications? Messages or photos not matching between devices? Too many inboxes to check? Any business software you're using or thinking about?"

## Closing the interview

1. **Read the Map back** section by section in plain sentences. Fix anything they correct.
2. **Rank the pains.** List what they told you in Part 1 plus anything painful that surfaced later. Ask them to pick the top three together. If they can't agree, each picks their own number one and both go on the list.
3. **List the open items.** Everything marked `not sure yet`, each with a one-line note on when it will matter.
4. **Check the Map for anything sensitive** against the list in `04-map-and-checklist.md`, and remove it.
5. **Save the Map** using the save steps in `04-map-and-checklist.md`. Confirm with them that it is saved before going on.
6. **Offer the break.** "That's the interview done. The next part takes about 15 to 20 minutes and it's where you two decide what to use. Carry on now, or pick it up later?" If later, mark the checklist and tell them exactly how to resume: start a new chat in the project and paste the starter message again, with both of them there.
